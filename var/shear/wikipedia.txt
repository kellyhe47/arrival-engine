'''Emmett Shear''' (born 1983) is an American [[Internet entrepreneur]] and investor.<ref>{{cite news|title=Forty Under 40: Emmett Shear, Twitch|url=https://www.bizjournals.com/sanfrancisco/news/2017/03/09/young-leaders-40-under-emmett-shear-twitch.html|accessdate=May 7, 2018|date=March 9, 2017}}</ref>{{Efn|Name appeared as "Emmett Sheer" in "The ESPN of Videogames", ''Forbes'' (paper), ppg. 36,40, 2 Nov 2013.}} He co-founded the live video platform [[Justin.tv]]. Shear became the [[chief executive officer]] of [[Twitch (service)|Twitch]] when it was spun off from Justin.tv, a position he held until March 2023. In 2011, he was appointed as a part-time partner at venture capital firm [[Y Combinator (company)|Y Combinator]].<ref name=":2">{{cite web|url=https://venturebeat.com/2011/06/13/y-combinator-partners-alums/|title=Y Combinator's latest partners are also alums|work=VentureBeat|date=June 13, 2011}}</ref> In November 2023, he was briefly the interim CEO of [[OpenAI]].<ref>{{Cite web |last=Hagey |first=Berber Jin, Deepa Seetharaman, Tom Dotan and Keach |title=Sam Altman Not Returning as CEO of OpenAI; Emmett Shear to Be Interim CEO |url=https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openai-leadership-hangs-in-balance-as-sam-altmans-counte-rebellion-gains-steam-47276fa8 |access-date=2023-11-20 |website=WSJ |language=en-US |archive-date=November 20, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231120042759/https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openai-leadership-hangs-in-balance-as-sam-altmans-counte-rebellion-gains-steam-47276fa8 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="altman-return">{{cite web |last1=Heath |first1=Alex |title=Breaking: Sam Altman to return as CEO of OpenAI |url=https://www.theverge.com/2023/11/22/23967223/sam-altman-returns-ceo-open-ai |website=The Verge |language=en |date=22 November 2023}}</ref>

He is the CEO of [[AI alignment]] startup Softmax.<ref>{{Cite web |title=https://x.com/eshear |url=https://x.com/eshear |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250307210110/https://x.com/eshear |archive-date=March 7, 2025 |access-date=2025-07-22 |website=X (formerly Twitter) |language=en |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=":3">{{Cite web |title=Softmax - About |url=https://www.softmax.com/about |access-date=2025-07-22 |website=www.softmax.com}}</ref>

== Career ==

=== Y Combinator ===

Shear and Justin Kan were part of [[Y Combinator]]'s first class in 2005.<ref name=":2" /> As part of Y Combinator, the two built a calendar application called Kiko, which they eventually sold on [[eBay]] for around $250,000 after [[Google Calendar]] was introduced.<ref name="buzzfeed"/><ref name=":1" />

=== Justin.tv ===
In March 2007, Shear and Justin Kan, along with [[Michael Seibel]] and [[Kyle Vogt]], launched [[Justin.tv]], a 24/7 [[Livestreaming|live video]] feed of Kan's life, broadcast via a [[webcam]] attached to his head.<ref>{{cite web|url=http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2007/03/30/MNG8OOUQI81.DTL&hw=justin+tv&sn=001&sc=1000|title=IT'S JUSTIN, LIVE! ALL DAY, ALL NIGHT! / S.F. startup puts camera on founder's head for real-time feed, and a star is born|work=San Francisco Chronicle|date=March 30, 2007|access-date=November 3, 2011|archive-date=December 18, 2008|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20081218105550/http://www.sfgate.com/cgi-bin/article.cgi?f=/c/a/2007/03/30/MNG8OOUQI81.DTL&hw=justin+tv&sn=001&sc=1000|url-status=dead}}</ref> It quickly obtained some media coverage. In October 2007, the site added the possibility for other users to host their own broadcast. Three years later, the platform had secured $7.2 million in venture capital funding and reported a monthly user base of approximately 31 million unique visitors.<ref name=":1">{{Cite web |last=Johnson |first=Eric |date=2014-07-05 |title=How Twitch's Founders Turned an Aimless Reality Show Into a Video Juggernaut |url=https://www.vox.com/2014/7/5/11628568/how-twitchs-founders-turned-an-aimless-reality-show-into-a-video |access-date=2024-06-29 |website=Vox |language=en-US}}</ref> Its gaming-oriented spin-off Twitch eventually became more popular,<ref name=":1" /> and Justin.tv was closed on August 5, 2014.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Kumparak |first=Greg |date=2014-08-05 |title=Justin.tv Shuts Down To Let The Company Focus On Twitch |url=https://techcrunch.com/2014/08/05/justin-tv-shuts-down-to-let-the-company-focus-on-twitch/ |access-date=2024-06-29 |website=TechCrunch |language=en-US}}</ref>

=== Twitch ===
After Justin.tv launched in 2007, the site quickly began building subject-specific content categories like Social, Tech, Sports, Entertainment, News & Events, and Gaming. Gaming, in particular, grew very fast and became the most popular content on the site.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://venturebeat.com/2011/03/10/justin-tv-esports-channel/|title=Live-streaming site Justin.tv buffing up for e-sports channels|work=VentureBeat|date=March 10, 2011|access-date=August 31, 2017|archive-date=August 9, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20170809131503/https://venturebeat.com/2011/03/10/justin-tv-esports-channel/|url-status=live}}</ref>

In June 2011,<ref>{{Cite news|title = The ESPN of Videogames|last = Ewalt|first = David M.|date = December 2, 2013|work = Forbes|page = 40|author-link = David M. Ewalt|url = https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidewalt/2013/11/13/the-espn-of-video-games|access-date = August 27, 2017|archive-date = August 3, 2017|archive-url = https://web.archive.org/web/20170803091943/https://www.forbes.com/sites/davidewalt/2013/11/13/the-espn-of-video-games/|url-status = live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web|url=https://thenextweb.com/media/2011/06/06/twitchtv-justin-tvs-killer-new-esports-project/|title=TwitchTV: Justin.tv's killer new esports project|author=Alex Wilhelm|date=June 6, 2011|work=The Next Web|access-date=June 18, 2018|archive-date=June 14, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180614221301/https://thenextweb.com/media/2011/06/06/twitchtv-justin-tvs-killer-new-esports-project/|url-status=live}}</ref> the company decided to spin off the gaming content under a separate brand and site. They named it [[TwitchTV]], inspired by the term [[twitch gameplay]]. On August 29, 2011, Shear became CEO of Justin.tv, and remained in that role as the company rebranded around Twitch in 2014, which had quickly become its core product.<ref>{{cite web|url=https://techcrunch.com/2011/08/29/after-a-hot-start-justin-tv-spins-off-socialcam-its-instagram-for-video/|title=After A Hot Start, Justin.tv Spins Off Socialcam, Its 'Instagram for Video'|work=TechCrunch|date=August 29, 2011|publisher=AOL|access-date=June 25, 2017|archive-date=December 17, 2017|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20171217005839/https://techcrunch.com/2011/08/29/after-a-hot-start-justin-tv-spins-off-socialcam-its-instagram-for-video/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite web |last1=Lawler |first1=Ryan |title=With More Than 20M Gamers Tuning In, Twitch Raises $15M From Bessemer Venture Partners |url=https://techcrunch.com/2012/09/19/twitch-15m-bessemer-venture-partners/ |website=TechCrunch |access-date=22 November 2023 |date=19 September 2012}}</ref>

On August 25, 2014, [[Amazon (company)|Amazon]] officially acquired Twitch for a reported $970,000,000.<ref>{{cite news|title=Here's Why Amazon Just Paid Nearly $1 Billion For A Site Where You Watch People Play Video Games|url=http://www.businessinsider.com/heres-why-amazon-paid-almost-1-billion-for-twitch-2014-8|accessdate=May 7, 2018|work=Business Insider|date=August 25, 2014|archive-date=May 7, 2018|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20180507154206/http://www.businessinsider.com/heres-why-amazon-paid-almost-1-billion-for-twitch-2014-8|url-status=live}}</ref>
[[File:Emmett shear manifest talk.jpg|alt=Emmett Shear speaking at Manifest 2023|thumb|Shear speaking at Manifest 2023]]
In March 2023, Shear announced that he was resigning as CEO, and that [[Dan Clancy|Daniel J. Clancy]] would take over.<ref>{{cite web | url=https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2023/03/16/16-years-of-twitch/ | title=16 Years of Twitch | access-date=March 17, 2023 | archive-date=March 17, 2023 | archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230317112620/https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2023/03/16/16-years-of-twitch | url-status=live }}</ref>

=== Investor Activities ===
Shear became a part-time partner at [[Y Combinator (company)|Y Combinator]] in June 2011, where he offered advice to the new startups in each batch.<ref>{{Cite web |last= |first= |date=June 13, 2011 |title=Welcome Sam, Garry, Emmett, and Justin |url=https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/welcome-sam-garry-emmett-and-justin |access-date=2024-06-30 |website=Y Combinator |language=en}}</ref><ref name=":2" />

=== OpenAI ===
On November 19, 2023, Shear was named as the interim CEO of OpenAI,<ref>{{cite news |last1=Efrati |first1=Amir |title=Emmett Shear Becomes Interim OpenAI CEO as Altman Talks Break Down |url=https://www.theinformation.com/articles/breaking-sam-altman-will-not-return-as-ceo-of-openai |access-date=20 November 2023 |work=The Information |date=20 November 2023 |archive-date=November 20, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231120054641/https://www.theinformation.com/articles/breaking-sam-altman-will-not-return-as-ceo-of-openai |url-status=live }}</ref> following the removal of [[Sam Altman]] by the board two days earlier. On November 21, an agreement was reached to reinstate Altman as CEO.<ref name="altman-return" /> It was previously reported that Shear had threatened to resign as CEO if the board could not provide evidence to support Altman's removal.<ref name="altman-return" />

Shear has publicly stated that he is concerned about the impact AI can have on civilization, estimating his "P(Doom)" ([[subjective probability]] of an [[Existential risk from artificial general intelligence|existential catastrophe from AI]] such as human extinction) as between 5 and 50 percent.<ref>{{Cite web |title=Tweet by @rowancheung of excerpt of podcast interview with Emmett Shear |url=https://twitter.com/rowancheung/status/1726473420299534491 |access-date=2023-11-20 |website=X (formerly Twitter) |language=en |archive-date=November 20, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231120061632/https://twitter.com/rowancheung/status/1726473420299534491 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite news |last1=Verma |first1=Pranshu |last2=Tiku |first2=Nitasha |last3=Vynck |first3=Gerrit De |date=2023-11-20 |title=OpenAI stands by its ouster of Sam Altman. Emmett Shear named interim CEO |language=en-US |newspaper=Washington Post |url=https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2023/11/20/emmett-shear-new-ceo-open-ai/ |access-date=2023-11-20 |issn=0190-8286 |archive-date=November 20, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231120083713/https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2023/11/20/emmett-shear-new-ceo-open-ai/ |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name="auto">{{Citation |title=Twitch Started as a Reality TV Show |url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ICnFtfN-sUc |access-date=2023-11-20 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20231120072210/https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ICnFtfN-sUc |archive-date=November 20, 2023 |url-status=live |language=en}}</ref>

=== Softmax ===
In March 2025, Shear co-founded Softmax, a startup focused on [[AI alignment]], alongside [[Adam Goldstein (author)|Adam Goldstein]] and David Bloomin.<ref name=":3" /><ref>{{Cite web |title=https://x.com/eshear/status/1905715502695256301 |url=https://x.com/eshear/status/1905715502695256301 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20250328214753/https://x.com/eshear/status/1905715502695256301 |archive-date=March 28, 2025 |access-date=2025-07-22 |website=X (formerly Twitter) |language=en |url-status=live }}</ref>

== Philanthropy ==

In March 2020, during the [[COVID-19 pandemic]], via Twitch, Shear donated the initial US$1 million to start a [[SF New Deal]], a non-profit organization which ordered meals from San Francisco eateries and delivered them to people in need. The organization was started by his Yale college classmate Leonore Estrada, who owned the Three Babes Bakeshop in San Francisco's Bayview neighborhood.<ref>{{Cite web |last=Duffett |first=Becky |date=2020-03-27 |title=A Tech Angel Just Gave an SF Pie Baker a Million Dollars |url=https://sf.eater.com/2020/3/27/21197153/three-babes-lenore-estrada-tech-angel-emmett-shear-twitch-million-dollars-sf-new-deal |access-date=2023-11-20 |website=Eater SF |language=en |archive-date=April 22, 2023 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20230422220439/https://sf.eater.com/2020/3/27/21197153/three-babes-lenore-estrada-tech-angel-emmett-shear-twitch-million-dollars-sf-new-deal |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{cite web |author=Katie Canales |title=Twitch CEO Emmett Shear just gave $1 million to a San Francisco baker to launch a nonprofit that will help small businesses amid a citywide shutdown to contain the coronavirus |url=https://www.businessinsider.com/twitch-ceo-san-francisco-coronavirus-shelter-in-place-2020-3 |accessdate=March 31, 2020 |publisher=[[Business Insider]] |archive-date=March 30, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200330214908/https://www.businessinsider.com/twitch-ceo-san-francisco-coronavirus-shelter-in-place-2020-3 |url-status=live }}</ref><ref name=":0" />

Intent

A score says two people have something in common. It does not say either of them needs anything. Intent is what the member is currently trying to do, derived from their own first-person material inside a rolling window — and it is the north star: among candidates that already clear the floor, intent decides which one is surfaced.

IDIntentDerived from
I1Deploying capitalInvestment announcements or thesis writing in the window; a firm role on record.
I2Raising or being backedFundraise language, a new entity, hiring posts for founding roles.
I3Building an institutionGovernance, structure or long-horizon writing sustained across the window.
I4Publishing a body of workRising cadence on one subject; a serialised or long-form project in progress.
I5Learning a domainA topic new to the record appearing in three or more items, none older than the window.
I6Giving accessOpen office hours, mentoring, an explicit standing offer to strangers.
I7Stepping backSustained fall in first-person output against that member's own baseline.
I8Being socialAttendance without an agenda: repeat visits in the window, event RSVPs, an open invitation to be approached. A stated intent, not a residual.
I0UnknownCoverage incomplete, or no item clears the evidence bar. Never inferred from a role, and never read as I8.

Two corroborating items minimum, each with a source and a date, or the intent is I0. I8 needs its own evidence like any other value — a member the engine knows nothing about is I0, never I8. A member may hold two intents; a third is treated as none, because a person doing three things is not doing any of them legibly. Window: 180 days, and intent decays out rather than being carried forward.

Intent classes

A pair's intent class is directional and decides rank, not points.

ClassExampleEffect on ranking
ComplementA is I2 raising, B is I1 deploying. A is I5 learning, B has done it.Surfaced first. S9 fires.
ParallelBoth I3, building the same kind of thing.Surfaced after complements. S9 does not fire.
NeutralNo relation between the two intents.Ranked on score alone.
OpenEither side is I8, being social.Ranked on score alone. An open member has said they are here to meet people, so the engine gets out of the way and lets the evidence pick.
GuardedA is I2 raising, B is I7 stepping back — and B is not I8.Ranked last, and the card names the asymmetry. Not suppressed.
UnknownEither side is I0.Ranked on score alone, and the card says coverage is incomplete.

Order of operations

StepWhy in this position
1Score every pair in the room, both directions, S1–S8.Evidence first. Intent cannot invent a reason to say out loud.
2Drop everything below the floor.Unchanged. Intent never rescues a pair with nothing measured.
3Class every surviving pair. I8 on either side means Open.Openness overrides the asymmetry rules. A member who came to be social has already answered the question the guard was protecting.
4Rank by class, in this order: complement, then parallel, then open, neutral and unknown together on score alone, then guarded last.The north star. A complement at 7 beats a neutral at 11; a guarded pair still appears if it is all the room has. Open, neutral and unknown share a rank because none of them carries a directional claim — the evidence decides.
5Add S9 to the displayed score where the class is complement.Display only. The floor is still evaluated without S9.
6Write the reason from the intent, not the overlap.“He is raising; she has funded four of these” is a sentence a host can say.

A complement at 7 beating a neutral at 11 is the whole point, and it is the part to argue with: it means the surfaced name is no longer the highest-scoring one, and the score on the card will sometimes look wrong to anyone reading it as a ranking.

What intent must never do

RuleBecause
Never rendered as a need“He is looking for funding” is a claim about a member's situation. “He announced a new company in March” is a sourced fact. The card says the second.
Never inferred from a role or a titleA partner at a firm is not necessarily deploying, and a founder is not necessarily raising. Role is not intent.
Never inferred from silenceI7 stepping back requires a measured fall against that member's own baseline, and unreached sources make it I0.
Never carried across a suppressionA withheld class cannot be laundered into an intent. If the evidence is suppressed, so is the intent it would imply.
Never used as a residualBeing social is a finding with its own evidence. Treating it as the default for members the engine could not read would collapse “here to meet people” into “we do not know” — the one distinction this product exists to keep.
Never shown to the member's faceIntent is the most inferential thing on the card. It survives the over-the-shoulder test only when it reads as what they published.

The floor

RuleValue
Threshold is evaluated on the score excluding S8yes
Displayed score includes S8yes
Names surfaced per arrival, at most1
Named candidates below the floor0
Candidates named on a withheld card0

Nothing is suppressed for intent. The only path that withholds a card is a failed hard gate, and it withholds the whole card rather than a candidate. The floor is set so that clearing it requires at least one specific signal, not an accumulation of broad ones. Below it the introduction has no reason the host can say out loud, and a weak introduction spends credibility the strong one will need. The value is tuning, so it lives in configuration and on the card that reports a miss — not here.

A topic held by 40% or more of the member base is excluded from S5. Genericity is measured once over the whole member base and stored — never recomputed from who is in the room, which made the score depend on room size.

Direction

Scoring is directional. A's interest in B is not B's interest in A, because S7 and S8 are both one-way: a citation has an author, and a status gradient has a direction.
